# 🦴 Anatomy of a prompt

A good prompt has 9 parts. Almost all of them come from your PRD.

| # | Part | What it says | Spot the Rip example |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Context | Who you are, what you're making | "I'm 13 and new to coding. Help me build a small web game." |
| 2 | Who it's for | The persona | "Mateo, 12, surfs at Dominical on weekends." |
| 3 | The problem | A real quote | "I know rips are dangerous but I can't see them." |
| 4 | The ONE thing | What must work | "Show a beach photo. The player taps where the rip is." |
| 5 | Step by step | What happens, in order | "Start → photo + 10-second timer → tap → right or wrong + one tip → next photo → score." |
| 6 | Done when | How you'll test it | "Tapping the rip shows green. Tapping elsewhere shows red. The timer stops at 0." |
| 7 | Rules | Limits | "One index.html. Plain HTML, CSS, JavaScript. Works on a phone. Comment each part." |
| 8 | Not yet | What to leave out | "No accounts, no sound, no leaderboard." |
| 9 | How to answer | What you want back | "Ask me up to 5 questions first. Build only #4. Then explain it in 5 sentences." |

## Short prompts for later

- **Fix:** "I did ___, expected ___, got ___. Error: [paste]. Fix only this and tell me what you changed."
- **Change:** "Change only ___. Don't touch anything else."
- **Explain:** "Explain lines ___ to ___ like I'm 12."
